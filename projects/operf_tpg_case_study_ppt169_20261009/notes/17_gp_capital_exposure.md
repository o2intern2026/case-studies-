In the structure diagram the GP is also an investor, so if investments fail, its own capital can be lost. The matrix sets carry beside GP invested capital. Carry offers potential performance compensation when there are qualifying profits and may simply be absent when there are losses. GP capital earns an investment return under the agreed terms when things go well and can be lost when they do not. The case notes TPG's roughly $500 million own commitment. So the manager has a profit motive and also a real downside exposure. That strengthens alignment, but it does not remove every conflict, and the actual sharing of losses depends on fund terms including any clawback. Question 2 has described the mechanisms.
