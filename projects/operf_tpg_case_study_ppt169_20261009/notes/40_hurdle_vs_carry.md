The two changes affect investors in different ways. A hurdle determines what investors receive before TPG earns carry: investors get the accrued preferred return first, although the hurdle does not guarantee that return. Catch-up determines how much of that advantage persists; with full catch-up TPG can recover its agreed profit share after investors receive the preference, and without catch-up investors keep more of the benefit. Lower carry changes the share of eligible profit TPG receives, but on its own it does not create a preferred return. Contemporary evidence gives a market reference: in Metrick and Yasuda's July 2007 sample, 93.1% of 144 buyout funds used hurdles and 8% was the most common rate, while the same terms table recorded no buyout funds with carry below 20%. That is a historical sample, not the whole market, and TPG VI's exact hurdle and catch-up terms remain unconfirmed. In this discussion LP means the investors and GP means TPG, and the model returns contributed capital, including contributions used for fees, before paying the preferred return and carry.
