The bars are illustrative annual management-fee revenue on the $19.8 billion fund: $297 million at the original 1.50%, $267.3 million after the cut to 1.35%, and $240.57 million if the fee base were also 10% smaller, which is a hypothetical. The fee cut works through the rate and the commitment reduction works through the base, and together they lower the fee burden. But neither turns the management fee into pay that depends on realised profits, so a size incentive can remain. These are uniform annual figures before offsets and costs, not actual receipts. Still, it would be wrong to describe the GP as collecting fees without bearing investment risk, and for that we go back to the manager's own capital in the structure diagram.
