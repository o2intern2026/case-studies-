Moving growth from 20% to 19% removes $2.84 million of value, essentially all of the $2.82 million base-case value; at exactly 19% the marginal NPV is about zero, minus $0.013 million unrounded. The decline is far larger than the roughly $0.66 million the fee cut adds to the marginal tranche. The chart shows the range: minus $20.05 million at 10% growth, minus $2.71 million at 18%, plus $2.82 million at the 20% base case and plus $19.29 million at 25%. At 18% the LP IRR of 14.06% is still positive but below the assumed 15% discount rate, so positive IRR can still mean negative NPV. At 10% growth, either hurdle lifts the marginal NPV only to minus $17.97 million, so better terms cannot rescue every weak case. We therefore need a defensible outlook for this additional exposure; the fee cut alone is not enough to justify keeping it. The economic margin is thin, so Alex's allocation and liquidity facts now tell us what else we must test before using scarce capacity for this additional $75 million.
