We have explained why TPG was willing to cut its fee; now look at the compensation mechanism being adjusted. Management fees support the team and its operating costs; they are charged periodically on the fee base agreed in the partnership agreement. Carried interest rewards distributable investment profits, determined under the distribution waterfall, and it is paid only when the agreed conditions are met. A salary-and-bonus analogy helps, with two cautions: the fee is not a personal salary, and carry is not an annual charge on fund size. In a whole-fund structure the steps are to return contributed capital, apply the agreed waterfall terms, and then allocate the remaining profits. Any preferred return, catch-up and other provisions depend on the LPA, and this illustration follows Metrick and Yasuda's description rather than TPG VI's actual contract. Because the two forms of pay rest on different bases, what the fee cut changes about incentives depends on the scale of fees, which is next.
