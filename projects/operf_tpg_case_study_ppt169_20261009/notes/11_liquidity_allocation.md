Two separate pressures. On the liquidity side, less predictable distributions meet binding commitments, which can create funding strain for some LPs. OPERF's own team, however, believed it could meet its calls, so the case for reducing cannot simply be that OPERF lacks cash. On the allocation side, the PE weight is PE value divided by total portfolio value. When public assets fall faster than private-equity marks adjust, the PE weight can rise passively, and that calls for a separate allocation judgment. We assume no fixed PE value here; it is a qualitative mechanism. The closing line is the one to keep: payment capacity and portfolio allocation are separate decisions. These different constraints shape how willing LPs are to keep supporting the fund, so now we turn the perspective back to TPG.
