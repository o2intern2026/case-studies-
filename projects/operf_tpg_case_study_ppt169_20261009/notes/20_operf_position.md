Two facts frame OPERF's position. Its private-equity allocation was 21.9% at the end of 2008 against a 16% target, so it was over-allocated. And its $750 million commitment to Fund VI is 2.5 times the $300 million it committed to each of Funds II to V. The case also reports that OPERF has enough cash to meet future capital calls. Treat that as a feasibility condition: it tells us OPERF can participate, not whether the investment is worth it. Allocation and investment value still have to be judged separately, so none of this on its own says reduce or retain. The two charts use independent scales.
