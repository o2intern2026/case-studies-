Now vary growth. At 10% asset growth the LP IRR is 6.42%, below the 15% model benchmark, yet the GP still receives carry worth about $533 million in present value; at 0% growth the IRR is negative 2.87% and carry is zero. The fee present value of $1,731 million is the same in every case. This shows that carry can arise when the LP's return is below the assumed discount rate; it does not show a breach of the real contract. The reason is that this model returns nominal capital and then shares profit, without requiring the LP to reach 15% first. The 10% case is still a positive IRR, not a nominal loss. And carry does move with performance, while the case records TPG's roughly $500 million co-investment, so this is not a claim that the GP bears no risk.
