# OPERF × TPG — Slide Naming & Navigation System

> **Purpose:** This section is a naming and navigation specification for PPT Master. Integrate it into the existing OPERF × TPG presentation plan. It defines **how to name and display** section-transition slides, category labels, and individual slide titles; it does **not** replace the five-part structure, change the analytical content, or request extra slides for every label.
>
> **Reference:** *Frontier Advisory — Dow's Bid for Rohm and Haas* presentation. Borrow its clear hierarchy of **question-led transitions → short topic labels → informative slide titles**, not its exact layout, colour palette, or wording.

## 1. Core principle: Three levels of naming

Use three distinct levels throughout the deck:

| Level | Element | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Level 1** | **Core question / transition slide** | Introduces the overarching question for a Part | `THE ECONOMIC QUESTION` + “What are the financial consequences for OPERF and TPG?” |
| **Level 2** | **Category label / small top tag** | Identifies the specific analytical topic of the current content slide | `RETURN ATTRIBUTION` |
| **Level 3** | **Main slide title** | States the specific point or conclusion of that slide | “How Fees and Carry Reduce LP Returns” |

**Do not confuse the three levels.** A category label is not a main title; a core question is not a label repeated on every slide. One category label may be reused across several consecutive content slides.

### Visual hierarchy (descriptive, not a locked design)

**Transition slide:**

- Small, prominent tag near the top: `THE ECONOMIC QUESTION`
- Thin divider or consistent framing treatment
- Large central question: *What are the financial consequences for OPERF and TPG?*
- Generous negative space; little or no supporting detail

**Regular analytical slide:**

- Small top tag: `RETURN ATTRIBUTION`
- Main headline below: `How Fees and Carry Reduce LP Returns`
- Analytical graphic/table/explanation below the headline
- Compact source or assumptions note where relevant

PPT Master should preserve the **structure and hierarchy**, while choosing the final typefaces, spacing, colours, and layout as part of the approved overall visual direction.

## 3. Level 1 — Five core-question transition slides

These are **proposed question-led transition titles**, written in the style of the Dow reference deck. Each introduces the subsequent Part; the following content slides answer the question.

| Part | Top tag | Main question (central headline) | Intended role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Part 1 — Li** | **THE INVESTMENT QUESTION** | **What is at stake in OPERF’s decision on TPG?** | Establish the decision, parties, and stakes without giving the final recommendation yet |
| **Part 2 — Li** | **THE STRATEGIC QUESTION** | **Why is TPG revising its terms amid the 2008 crisis?** | Explain the market shock, GP motivation, and proposed changes |
| **Part 3 — Li** | **THE INCENTIVE QUESTION** | **Do management fees and carry align GP and LP interests?** | Explain compensation mechanisms and potential misalignment |
| **Part 4 — Alex** | **THE ECONOMIC QUESTION** | **What are the financial consequences for OPERF and TPG?** | Quantify base-case fund economics, LP/GP outcomes, and the proposed fee cut |
| **Part 5 — Jimmy** | **THE NEGOTIATION QUESTION** | **What should OPERF negotiate—and should it reduce its commitment?** | Assess hurdle/carry terms, commitment reduction, and final recommendations |

### Narrative progression

`What is at stake?` → `Why change the terms?` → `Are incentives aligned?` → `What is the financial impact?` → `What should OPERF do?`

### Usage rules

- Prefer **one core-question transition at the start of each Part**, subject to slide-budget and the existing plan. If the current plan already has an equivalent divider, **replace/relabel that slide rather than adding a duplicate**.
- Keep transition slides sparse and question-led. Do not place model tables or crowded bullet lists on these slides.
- **Part 1 vs Part 5:** Part 1 sets up *the decision to be made*; Part 5 provides *the decision recommendation*. Do not repeat the same framing or conclusions in both.
- **Part 3 vs Part 4:** Part 3 explains *the incentive mechanism*; Part 4 quantifies *the financial consequences*. Avoid duplicating the same numerical analysis.

## 4. Level 2 — Category labels / small top tags

These are the **canonical category labels** to be used on ordinary content slides. There are 17 proposed labels across five Parts. **This is a label vocabulary, not a request for 17 new slides.** Reuse a label for related consecutive slides.

### Part 1 — Li: Case Setup and Decision

**Core question:** `THE INVESTMENT QUESTION`

| Category label | Apply to slides about… |
|---|---|
| **CASE BACKGROUND** | Key participants, case facts, prior context, and the relationship between OPERF and TPG |
| **THE DECISION AT HAND** | The decision OPERF faces, the practical alternatives, and why the decision matters |

### Part 2 — Li: 2008 Crisis and TPG’s Motivation

**Core question:** `THE STRATEGIC QUESTION`

| Category label | Apply to slides about… |
|---|---|
| **THE 2008 CRISIS** | The financial crisis, liquidity environment, and changing private-equity conditions |
| **TPG'S MOTIVATION** | Why TPG may want to revise fees and commitments and how the proposal responds to conditions |
| **PROPOSED RESTRUCTURING** | The proposed fee structure and commitment changes, presented neutrally and precisely |

### Part 3 — Li: GP/LP Compensation and Misalignment

**Core question:** `THE INCENTIVE QUESTION`

| Category label | Apply to slides about… |
|---|---|
| **COMPENSATION STRUCTURE** | Management fees versus carried interest; how, when, and on what base compensation is earned |
| **GP–LP INCENTIVES** | Economic incentives of GPs versus LPs and how the terms may shape behaviour |
| **INCENTIVE MISALIGNMENT** | Conflicts, trade-offs, and potential divergence between the two parties’ interests |

### Part 4 — Alex: OPERF’s Position and Financial Analysis

**Core question:** `THE ECONOMIC QUESTION`

| Category label | Apply to slides about… |
|---|---|
| **OPERF'S POSITION** | OPERF’s allocation, constraints, and financial position relevant to the TPG decision |
| **FUND ECONOMICS** | Q3 prescribed assumptions, capital calls, fund cash flows, distributions, and base-case mechanics |
| **RETURN ATTRIBUTION** | Gross-to-net return differences, fee drag, carried interest, and the LP/GP value split |
| **FEE REDUCTION IMPACT** | Q4: management fee reduction from 1.50% to 1.35% and its impact on LP/GP outcomes |
| **SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS** | The effect of changing selected model inputs and clarifying which conclusions are robust |

**Example label progression within Part 4:**

| Illustrative content slide | Top category label |
|---|---|
| OPERF investment allocation / decision constraints | `OPERF'S POSITION` |
| Base-case modelling assumptions | `FUND ECONOMICS` |
| Ten-year capital calls, asset growth, and distributions | `FUND ECONOMICS` |
| Gross-to-net returns / fee and carry waterfall | `RETURN ATTRIBUTION` |
| Committed-capital fee base and fee drag | `RETURN ATTRIBUTION` |
| Financial implications of the 15 bp fee cut | `FEE REDUCTION IMPACT` |
| Scenario or parameter sensitivities | `SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS` |

**Model interpretation guardrails (retain from the analytical plan):**

- Describe 15% as the **assumed discount rate / model benchmark**, not an established OPERF investment-policy “required return.”
- Identify NPV as **NPV at model inception; 15% assumed discount rate** when that is the valuation convention; distinguish it from a later commitment-reduction decision.
- State that **no hurdle** is a **Q3 base-case modelling assumption** and should not be presented as a verified contractual term for TPG.
- Clearly differentiate **20% assumed invested-asset growth** from **LP net IRR**; they are not the same measure.

### Part 5 — Jimmy: Recommendation and Negotiation Strategy

**Core question:** `THE NEGOTIATION QUESTION`

| Category label | Apply to slides about… |
|---|---|
| **NEGOTIATION LEVERS** | Negotiable economic terms, priorities, trade-offs, and decision criteria |
| **HURDLE & CARRY** | Q5: 8% hurdle, catch-up mechanics, and 15% versus 20% carry |
| **COMMITMENT DECISION** | Q6: whether OPERF should reduce its commitment by 10%, including economic and strategic considerations |
| **FINAL RECOMMENDATION** | Synthesised answer, negotiation priorities, commitment advice, and action plan |

**Do not imply** that the proposed 8% hurdle necessarily lowers the GP’s final carry in a full 100% catch-up structure; explain the applicable distribution mechanics and conditions.

## 5. Level 3 — Main slide title conventions

Category labels state **where the analysis is**; main titles should convey **what the audience should understand**.

| Weak, generic title | Better, informative title |
|---|---|
| Cash Flow Analysis | Capital Is Called Before Distributions Begin |
| Management Fees | Management Fees Are Charged on Committed, Not Invested, Capital |
| Fee and Carry | How Fees and Carry Reduce LP Returns |
| Proposed Fee Reduction | A 15 bp Fee Cut Improves LP Economics *(use only if supported by the model)* |
| Negotiation | Which Terms Can OPERF Realistically Improve? |
| Recommendation | The Recommended Course of Action for OPERF |

### Headline rules

1. Use natural, professional English; prefer concise declarative statements for analytical slides.
2. Do not overclaim: where the finding depends on an assumption or scenario, say so in the headline or nearby note.
3. Keep acronyms, units, and terms consistent across the deck: `GP`, `LP`, `OPERF`, `TPG`, `bp`, `carry`, `commitment`, `IRR`, `NPV`.
4. Do not force every slide to have a conclusion. Model-input, methodology, and setup slides may use clear descriptive titles.
5. For chart-led slides, let the chart substantiate the title; retain footnotes, data sources, and modelling qualifications as necessary.

## 6. Optional context-specific label variants

Dow sometimes combines a subject with a time or event (e.g., `THE DEAL · JULY 10, 2008`). Our deck may occasionally do the same when context materially helps navigation.

| Canonical category | Optional context-specific label |
|---|---|
| `THE 2008 CRISIS` | `MARKET CONTEXT · 2008` |
| `FUND ECONOMICS` | `FUND ECONOMICS · BASE CASE` |
| `FEE REDUCTION IMPACT` | `FEE REDUCTION · 15 BP` |
| `COMMITMENT DECISION` | `COMMITMENT · 10% REDUCTION` |

**Use variants sparingly.** Keep the canonical label by default; do not create a fresh variant for every slide. If a variant is used, keep naming and capitalisation consistent for that type of analysis.

## 7. PPT Master implementation instructions

1. **Preserve the approved deck structure:** five Parts, three presenters, and coverage of Q1–Q6. Treat this file as a naming overlay, not a new slide-by-slide outline.
2. **Map existing slide content to the labels.** Assign one relevant Level-2 category to each ordinary analytical slide. Reuse categories across adjacent slides when the subject is unchanged.
3. **Introduce each Part with its Level-1 core-question treatment** where the existing plan and time budget allow. Reuse or relabel existing section dividers instead of adding redundant pages.
4. **Use Level-3 titles as informative headlines.** Titles should align with the analysis and reflect the actual workbook results, not merely describe what type of chart appears on the page.
5. **Maintain a consistent visual navigation system** across Li, Alex, and Jimmy: placement, typography, case, label dimensions, and spacing should be coherent.
6. **Avoid visual overloading:** The small tag must remain subordinate to the main headline and data; do not create oversized banners on regular slides.
7. **Do not hard-code the reference deck's palette.** Follow the separate approved visual-direction/colour section of the presentation plan.
8. **Preserve analytical precision**, especially the model assumptions and timing distinctions in Parts 4–5; do not allow shorter headlines to distort financial meaning.
9. **No automatic slide-count expansion:** Category labels are reusable navigational elements, not additional slide requests.

### Final consistency checklist

- [ ] Five Parts remain intact and in the agreed order.
- [ ] Each Part has an appropriate core question or equivalent existing section divider.

- [ ] Main titles communicate content clearly and do not overstate the analysis.
- [ ] Q1–Q6 are covered without duplicating content between adjacent Parts.
- [ ] Financial assumptions, timing, and sources remain visible where needed.
- [ ] Naming treatment aligns with the deck’s separately approved visual direction.
